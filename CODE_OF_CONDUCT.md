# Code of Conduct

This code of conduct is adapted from [The Whitaker Lab](https://github.com/WhitakerLab/Onboarding/blob/master/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) 
code of conduct, which we consider gold standard.

We value the participation of every member of our community and want to ensure
that every contributor has an enjoyable and fulfilling experience. Accordingly,
everyone who participates in any DataSciBC project is expected to show respect
and courtesy to other community members at all time.

Ed Palmer, as head of the DataSciBC (aka "Palmer-Lab"), and all lab members, are
dedicated to a positive, respectful, and harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of gender, gender identity and expression, sexual
orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, age or religion.

As a community, **we do not tolerate bullying or harassment by and/or of members
of our community in any form**.

We are particularly motivated to support new and/or anxious collaborators, those
who would consider themselves under-represented in our field, and anyone at any
stage who are looking to learn and develop new skills.

To make clear what is expected, we ask all members of the community to conform
to the following Code of Conduct:

- All communication---online and in person---should be appropriate for a 
  professional audience including people of many different backgrounds. 
- Be respectful to others. Do not insult or put down other contributors.
  It can take a lot of courage to showcase your work to others and await
  critical feedback. We seek to make that experience a positive one.

**To report an issue** please contact [Ed Palmer](https://github.com/DocEd).
All communication will be treated as confidential.

## Flexible working hours

This sentence is stolen from [Bastian Greshake's](https://github.com/gedankenstuecke) 
email footer and forms the basis of the DataSciBC's policy on flexible working:

> While I may be sending this email outside my normal office hours, I have no
expectation to receive a reply outside yours.

The hours that members of the lab choose to put in is up to them. This is a
*voluntary endeavour*, and we understand that time is both a valuable and precious
commodity. We are each welcome to send work-related communications or pull
requests over the weekend or late at night, but no lab members are required or
expected to reply to them outside of "typical" working hours. The DataSciBC
maintains a whatsapp group for more casual communication, and these same
principles apply. Members are welcome to mute, or even leave and rejoin when
convenient.

Ideally, all lab members will have some time each week to overlap with Ed in
order to stay in touch on any challenges or successes. The Thursday morning
meeting is the best opportunity for this.
