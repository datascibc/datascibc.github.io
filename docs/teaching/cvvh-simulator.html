Assumptions and limits of the model

  • Steady state, one-dimensional: the filter is divided into 120 axial segments, and all fibres are assumed to behave identically (no flow maldistribution).
  • Fibre geometry is fixed at 200 µm internal diameter and 25 cm length; fibre number is derived from surface area. Real filters vary.
  • Plasma proteins are fully reflected (σ = 1) for oncotic purposes; the albumin sieving figure is used only to estimate losses.
  • Membrane permeability is a single lumped value. Protein adsorption, the secondary membrane, concentration polarisation and progressive clotting are represented only by lowering it; set it low to model a fouled filter.
  • Solutes are tracked in plasma water only. Movement of urea between red cells and plasma, protein binding (beyond what the sieving coefficient already reflects) and solute in replacement fluid are ignored.
  • Effluent-side pressure is uniform; no diffusion occurs (pure convection, no dialysate); temperature effects on viscosity are ignored.
  • Access pressure is a rough linear estimate; in reality it depends on catheter size, position and patient factors.
  • The 24-hour projection is single-pool with no generation, so it shows the shape of removal, not a predicted lab value.
  • Sieving coefficients are typical literature values for high-flux synthetic membranes and vary between membranes and studies.
  • Lactate: one compartment with constant production and a fixed endogenous clearance. Real lactate metabolism is saturable and falls in liver failure and shock. Replacement fluid is assumed to be bicarbonate-buffered and lactate-free.
  • Phosphate: the pseudo-one-compartment model was derived in maintenance haemodialysis, not critical illness, and assumes the mobilisable tissue pool never runs down. Over days of CVVH, body stores do fall, and without supplementation hypophosphataemia develops.

An educational model for building intuition. It is not a prescribing tool.
